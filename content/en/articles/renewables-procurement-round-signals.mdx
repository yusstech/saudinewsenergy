When bid tariffs cluster, the auction stops being about price.

The Kingdom's utility-scale solar programme has reached that point. Successive rounds have compressed the spread between competitive bids to a margin that is small relative to the cost of a delayed connection — which means the variable that decides project economics has moved from the tariff sheet to the grid.

## The number behind the number

A tariff is a price per megawatt-hour delivered. Delivery is the operative word. A plant that is complete but waiting on a connection is earning nothing, and a plant connected to a constrained corridor is earning less than its nameplate implies.

That makes two non-price terms decisive: the date the connection is available, and the curtailment exposure once it is.

## What this changes for developers

Developers who can carry connection risk — because they have the balance sheet, or because their scope includes the associated network works — can bid differently from those who cannot. Over several rounds that tends to concentrate awards rather than distribute them.

The programme-level question is whether transmission build-out keeps pace well enough that the distinction stops mattering.
