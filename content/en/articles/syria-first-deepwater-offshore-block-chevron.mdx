Exploration agreements are announced far more often than they are acted on.

A memorandum of understanding is a cheap document. It commits the parties to look at something, and looking is the least expensive thing an oil company does. The industry accumulates memoranda that never become wells.

So the date that matters in Syria's offshore programme is not February.

It is **11 May 2026**, when the [Syrian Petroleum Company](https://sana.sy/en/economic/2309563/) announced it had received an official notice from Chevron to proceed with the selected offshore block, in cooperation with UCC Holding.

A notice to proceed is not an agreement to consider. It is an instruction to begin, and it is where money starts moving.

## Three months from memorandum to instruction

The sequence ran quickly by the standards of offshore exploration:

| Date | Stage |
| --- | --- |
| 4 February 2026 | Memorandum of understanding signed with Chevron International and Power International Holding |
| 26 March 2026 | SPC chief executive discusses cooperation with Chevron at CERAWeek, Houston |
| 10 April 2026 | SPC says Chevron has confirmed it will proceed with implementation |
| 11 May 2026 | Official notice to proceed received on the selected block |
| Summer 2026 | Technical and field operations scheduled to begin |

Roughly three months separate the memorandum from the notice to proceed.

For an offshore prospect in a country with no deep-water history, no established offshore licensing round and no prior wells in its territorial waters, that is a compressed timeline.

## What has to happen in that gap

The interval between "we will study this" and "begin work" is not administrative delay. It contains most of the intellectual work of exploration.

A block has to be selected — which means seismic data has to exist, be acquired or be reprocessed, then interpreted well enough to identify a prospect worth drilling within it. Deep-water seismic interpretation is slow and expensive, and it is the stage at which most prospects are quietly abandoned.

Drilling equipment has to be secured. Deep-water rigs are a globally constrained market with long lead times; a rig is not something a project acquires on short notice.

Commercial terms have to be settled, and for Syria that involves a legal and sanctions environment that was materially different eighteen months earlier.

Compressing all of that into a quarter suggests the technical evaluation drew on data that already existed rather than starting from a blank map.

## Why "deep water" is the operative phrase

Syrian Petroleum Company chief executive **Yousef Qablawy** described the project as Syria's first deep-water exploration.

The distinction is not decorative. Deep water changes what a well costs and what it requires.

Shallow-water wells can be drilled from fixed structures resting on the seabed. Deep water cannot: it requires dynamically positioned drilling vessels that hold station without anchors, subsea equipment rated for the pressure at depth, and specialised well-control systems.

A shallow-water well and a deep-water well in the same basin can differ by an order of magnitude in cost.

That is why the notice to proceed carries the weight it does. It commits to a category of spending that a memorandum does not.

## The onshore track is separate and further along

The offshore work is not the only upstream activity.

The Syrian oil company also contracted **ConocoPhillips** and **Novatara Energy** to develop a number of existing onshore gas fields and explore for new ones.

That is a different kind of project with a different risk profile. Existing fields have known reservoirs, existing wells and existing production histories. Work there is largely rehabilitation — restoring wells and surface facilities to service — rather than discovery.

Onshore rehabilitation produces gas sooner. Offshore exploration might produce more, later, or nothing at all.

Running both is the standard shape of an upstream portfolio: near-term production from known assets funds long-dated exploration, and the exploration is what determines whether there is a business in a decade.

## What is still unknown

The public record does not identify the block, its water depth, its size, or the volumes the partners believe it may hold.

That reticence is normal at this stage and should not be read as evasion. Prospect specifics are commercially sensitive before drilling, and pre-drill resource estimates are among the least reliable numbers in the industry — routinely wrong by large factors in both directions.

What is on the record is the sequence: a memorandum in February, a notice to proceed in May, operations scheduled for the summer.

Whether the block holds anything is a question only a well answers, and the first one has not been drilled.

## The stage that was actually cleared

Syria's offshore ambitions have been discussed for years without a well.

What changed in 2026 was not the geology, which is as it has always been. It was that an international operator moved from evaluating a prospect to instructing work on it — and that the commercial and legal conditions permitting a US major to do so existed at all.

The block may disappoint. Most do.

But the stage that has historically stopped Syrian offshore projects was never the drilling. It was getting to the point where someone was willing to pay for it.
