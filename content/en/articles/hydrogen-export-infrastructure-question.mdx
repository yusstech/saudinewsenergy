Hydrogen is difficult to ship. That single fact shapes most of what follows in an export project.

At atmospheric conditions hydrogen is diffuse, and liquefying it is energy-intensive and cold — around 20 kelvin. The practical route to a distant buyer is conversion to ammonia, which is already shipped at scale in existing vessels and terminals, then either used as ammonia or cracked back to hydrogen at the destination.

## The energy cost of the round trip

Each step in that chain costs energy. Synthesising ammonia consumes some of the hydrogen's energy content; shipping consumes more; cracking it back consumes more again. A project's headline electrolyser capacity therefore says relatively little about how much usable hydrogen arrives at a buyer's gate.

## Why offtake decides

Because the chain is long and capital-intensive, financial close depends less on capacity than on whether someone has contracted to buy the output for long enough to underwrite it.

That is the number worth watching when a hydrogen project is announced: not the gigawatts, but the term and volume of the offtake behind them.
