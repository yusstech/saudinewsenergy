A refining margin is a spread, not a price, and spreads move for reasons the headline crude number does not show.

Product cracks in the Arab Gulf have held firmer than the seasonal pattern would ordinarily imply. Part of that is demand-side: summer power-sector fuel consumption changes how much product stays in the domestic system rather than reaching the export market.

## Not every product moves together

Margin strength is rarely uniform across the barrel. A firm middle-distillate crack alongside a soft one at the light end tells a different operational story than broad strength — it points to a demand shift rather than a supply constraint.

Readers tracking this should watch the individual cracks rather than a blended margin, which averages exactly the information that matters.
