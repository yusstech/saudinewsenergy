Renewable capacity is the number that gets announced. Transmission is the number that decides when it counts.

A solar plant that cannot reach a demand centre is a plant that produces curtailment reports. The Kingdom's renewable programme has moved fast enough that the binding constraint in several regions is no longer how quickly generation can be built, but how quickly the high-voltage corridors to carry it can be routed, permitted and energised.

## Long-lead infrastructure behaves differently

A transmission corridor is not a faster or slower version of a generation project. It is a different kind of undertaking.

Route and topographical engineering, wayleave and permits, and access-track construction all precede the first tower. On a long overhead line these can occupy a substantial share of the programme before any steel is erected — and none of them compress well, because they depend on landowners, regulators and terrain rather than on procurement.

## Communications are part of the line

One consequence is visible in how these projects are scoped. Protection, telecommunications, SCADA integration and fibre testing sit inside the same EPC package as the towers and conductors, rather than following as a separate contract.

That is not administrative tidiness. A line cannot be handed over as operational until the protection at each end can talk to the protection at the other, which means the fibre has to be spliced, tested and terminated before energisation, not after it.

## What to watch

The useful signal in the next round of awards is not headline capacity. It is how much of the announced programme has a corresponding transmission scope already tendered — and how much is waiting on a corridor that has not yet been routed.
